Introduction

Heuristic evaluation (HE) is a usability inspection method: instead of recruiting end users, a small group of evaluators inspects an interface on their own and judges it against a short list of recognized usability principles. The method was popularized by Jakob Nielsen and Rolf Molich in the early 1990s, and it remains one of the fastest, cheapest ways to surface usability problems in a design before (or alongside) user testing. In this course it serves as your bridge from abstract design principles to concrete, defensible critique of real interfaces.

The subtopics here frame four questions. What is HE, procedurally — who evaluates, how many evaluators, how findings are merged? Where did it come from, and how does it relate to other inspection methods such as cognitive walkthroughs? When should you reach for it — early in design, between usability tests, or as a quick audit of an existing product? And what are its limits — evaluators are not users, findings can be subjective, and HE tends to catch surface-level problems more reliably than deep task or workflow failures.

As you watch, pay attention to process details you will need to reproduce in your own evaluation: evaluating independently before discussing, documenting each issue with the heuristic it violates and where it occurs, consolidating duplicate findings across evaluators, and assigning severity. Also note where each video says HE should not stand alone — that caveat is part of understanding the method, not a footnote to it.
