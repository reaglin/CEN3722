Introduction

Building a working app is only half the job of shipping it. Before anything reaches a user, both the Apple App Store and Google Play require a package of graphical and textual assets: an icon in specific sizes, screenshots sized for multiple devices, an optional preview video, a title and subtitle, a short and long description, keyword or category metadata, a content rating, and a link to a privacy policy. These assets are not decoration — they are the interface between your product and a person deciding, in a few seconds, whether to install it.

This topic treats the store listing as a designed interface in its own right. The same HCI principles you have applied to screens apply here: visual consistency and branding in the icon, legibility and information hierarchy in screenshot captions, honest and accessible descriptions, and clear disclosure of what data you collect. App Store Optimization (ASO) is the practice of tuning the searchable and persuasive parts of that listing so the right users find your app and choose to install it.

The three videos below approach ASO from different angles: a conceptual introduction, a strategy overview from a major mobile marketing company, and a hands-on walkthrough using an ASO tool. As you watch, note the recurring vocabulary — keywords, conversion rate, impressions, installs — and pay attention to the distinction between the metadata fields that are indexed for search and the visual assets that drive conversion. Also notice where platform differences between Apple and Google are mentioned, since those differences will shape how you prepare your own submission assets.
