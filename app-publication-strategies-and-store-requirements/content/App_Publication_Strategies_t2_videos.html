Introduction

Publishing an app to the Apple App Store is less a single button press than a sequence of administrative, technical, and design steps. Before any code ships, a developer (or organization) must enroll in the Apple Developer Program, generate certificates and provisioning profiles so that builds are cryptographically signed, create an app record in App Store Connect, upload a build through Xcode, and supply metadata and marketing assets that meet Apple's specifications. Only then does the app enter App Review, where human reviewers check it against the App Store Review Guidelines.

This matters in a human-computer interfaces course because Apple's requirements are, in large part, interface requirements. Screenshot dimensions, app icons, privacy disclosures, age ratings, and accessibility information are all part of how users encounter and judge your product before they ever launch it. Many common rejections — broken links, placeholder content, misleading screenshots, missing privacy details, poor usability on certain devices — are failures of interface preparation rather than failures of engineering.

As you watch the videos below, pay attention to the order of operations: what has to exist in App Store Connect before a build can be uploaded, what Xcode handles automatically versus what you must configure, and where beta testing (TestFlight) fits relative to final review submission. Note also every place a video mentions an asset, a description field, or a compliance question, and start a checklist you can later apply to your own interface project.

About this video

A step-by-step walkthrough from the official Flutter channel covering what it takes to move a finished app to the iOS App Store. Based on its title and description, it breaks the process into roughly seven stages, which typically include registering a bundle identifier, setting up signing in Xcode, creating the app listing in App Store Connect, building a release archive, uploading it, and submitting for review. The Flutter framing is incidental — the App Store side of the workflow is the same regardless of how the app was built.

Why this video

It gives students a compact, end-to-end mental model of the submission pipeline from a reputable first-party channel, touching enrollment, code signing, App Store Connect setup, and review submission in a single short video.
