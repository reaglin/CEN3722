Introduction

Publishing an Android app is not simply a matter of uploading a file. Google Play requires a registered developer account, a properly built and signed release artifact (typically an Android App Bundle), a complete store listing with text and graphic assets, declarations about what your app does with user data, and a release strategy that moves the app through internal, closed, open, and finally production tracks. Each of these steps has rules attached, and failing any one of them can delay or block a release.

In this course, the submission process matters because it is where interface design decisions meet real-world distribution constraints. Screenshots, feature graphics, short and full descriptions, and permission disclosures are all user-facing interface elements — they are the first interaction a user has with your product, and they are judged by reviewers against published policy. Understanding the pipeline also helps you plan your project: signing keys, package names, and privacy declarations are easier to get right at the start than to retrofit.

As you watch these videos, pay attention to three things: the sequence of steps in the Play Console and what information is required at each one; the difference between build-time concerns (signing, bundles, versioning) and listing-time concerns (metadata, assets, content rating); and how policy requirements — especially around permissions and user disclosure — shape what you can legitimately ask of users. Note that Play Console interfaces and policies change over time, so treat the walkthroughs as a map of the process and confirm current details against Google's official documentation.
