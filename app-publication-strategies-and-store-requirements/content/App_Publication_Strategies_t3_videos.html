Introduction

Publishing a Windows application to the Microsoft Store is a structured process with four main stages: registering a developer account in Microsoft Partner Center, packaging your app (most commonly as an MSIX package), building a store listing with the required metadata and visual assets, and passing certification against Microsoft Store policies. Each stage has concrete requirements — an identity-verified account, a signed and correctly manifested package, screenshots and descriptions at specified sizes and lengths, and content that complies with the Store Policies and Developer Agreement.

This matters in an HCI course because the store listing is often the first interface a user encounters. Your icons, screenshots, short description, and accessibility information shape whether someone installs your app at all, and store requirements effectively impose a baseline of usability, accessibility, and transparency on the product itself. Learning the Microsoft process also gives you a point of comparison with the Apple App Store and Google Play, which solve the same problems with different rules.

As you watch the videos below, note that they come from Microsoft's own developer conference sessions, so they present the publishing pipeline from the platform owner's perspective. Look for the sequence of steps from code to published app, the specific listing elements that influence discoverability and conversion, and the policy and business-model changes that define the current Store. Keep in mind that these sessions are a few years old: always confirm exact size limits, fees, and policy clauses against current Microsoft Partner Center documentation before you submit.
