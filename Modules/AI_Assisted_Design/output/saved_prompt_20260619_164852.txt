  •   Describe the wireframe layout in terms of component hierarchy, spatial relationships, and interactive behaviors.
  •   AI-generated scaffolds provide a starting structure that designers can refine rather than building from scratch.
  •   Separating layout generation from styling prompts allows for cleaner, more maintainable output.
  •   Validate the generated prototype against the original wireframe before investing time in further refinement.
  • Rapid Component Generation
  •   AI can generate framework-specific components including React, Vue, Svelte, or Web Components on demand.
  •   Generated components should be reviewed for prop structure, event handling, and state management correctness.
  •   Requesting unit test stubs alongside component generation supports quality assurance from the start.
  •   Saving and annotating effective component prompts creates a reusable prompt library for future projects.
  • Iterating on Prototypes with AI Assistance
  •   Frame iteration prompts around observed usability issues and desired interaction outcomes.
  •   AI can generate multiple design variations for the same component, supporting design comparison and A/B testing.
  •   Use AI to quickly implement accessibility improvements identified during usability evaluations.
  •   Track prompt-driven changes in version control to maintain a clear record of design evolution.
  • Balancing Speed and Design Quality
  •   Establish a review checklist for AI-generated prototype components covering accessibility, design system alignment, and code clarity.
  •   Prioritize quality reviews at prototype milestones rather than reviewing every incremental AI-generated change.
  •   Refactor AI-generated code periodically to remove redundancies and align with evolving design system conventions.
  •   Communicate to stakeholders that AI-generated prototypes are exploratory artifacts, not production-ready deliverables.
  • Integrating AI Tools into Team Workflows
  •   Define team conventions for documenting AI-assisted contributions in commit messages and design notes.
  •   Maintain a shared prompt library so that all team members benefit from collectively refined prompting strategies.
  •   Assign human reviewers for all AI-generated outputs before they are merged into shared project branches.
  •   Rotate AI-assisted tasks to ensure all team members develop proficiency with the tools.

TOPIC 2: Maintaining Design Quality in AI-Assisted Projects
  • Defining Design Quality in the AI Context
  •   Design quality criteria include usability, accessibility, visual consistency, performance, and user need alignment.
  •   AI tools do not inherently optimize for user experience; they optimize for pattern-matching to training data.
  •   Quality standards should be documented in a project design brief before AI-assisted generation begins.
  •   Regularly revisiting quality criteria throughout the project prevents gradual drift from the original design intent.
  • Heuristic Evaluation of AI-Generated Interfaces
  •   Apply heuristic evaluation systematically to AI-generated prototypes at each major iteration checkpoint.
  •   Focus especially on error handling, feedback mechanisms, and consistency heuristics, which AI often underserves.
  •   Document heuristic violations as specific, actionable prompts for the next AI-assisted revision cycle.
  •   Involve multiple evaluators to counteract individual blind spots when reviewing AI-generated interfaces.
  • Accessibility Auditing of AI-Generated Code
  •   Run automated accessibility checkers such as axe or Lighthouse on every AI-generated prototype before user testing.
  •   Manually test keyboard navigation, focus management, and screen reader announcements in interactive components.
  •   Document accessibility issues with reference to specific WCAG success criteria to guide targeted AI revision prompts.
  •   Treat accessibility remediation as a non-negotiable quality gate, not an optional polish step.
  • Code Review Practices for AI-Generated Output
  •   Review AI-generated code for security vulnerabilities such as unsanitized inputs or improper dependency usage.
  •   Check semantic HTML correctness, ensuring elements are used according to their intended purpose.
  •   Evaluate performance implications of AI-generated code, including unnecessary re-renders or large bundle contributions.
  •   Require that AI-generated contributions be traceable to a documented prompt for accountability and reproducibility.
  • Preserving the Designer's Voice and Intent
  •   Maintain a living design rationale document that records the reasoning behind key design decisions.
  •   Actively compare AI-generated suggestions against user research insights to test their appropriateness.
  •   Reject or revise AI outputs that produce conventional solutions where the user research calls for differentiated approaches.
  •   Regularly revisit original user personas and scenarios to keep AI-assisted development anchored to user needs.
  Notes: Addresses strategies and evaluation practices that ensure AI-generated outputs meet human-centered design standards. Covers review processes, quality criteria, and the designer's role in critically evaluating AI contributions.

TOPIC 3: Ethical Considerations and Professional Responsibility
  • Attribution and Transparency in AI-Assisted Work
  •   Disclose AI tool usage in project documentation, design handoffs, and academic submissions as appropriate.
  •   Attribute AI-assisted contributions clearly in version control commit messages and design notes.
  •   Transparency about AI use supports team trust and enables accurate assessment of project contributions.
  •   Develop personal norms for AI disclosure that align with both institutional policies and professional ethics.
  • Bias and Representation in AI-Generated Interfaces
  •   Critically evaluate AI-generated interface content and imagery for unintended cultural or demographic bias.
  •   Test generated interfaces with diverse user groups to surface bias that may not be apparent to the design team.
  •   Use inclusive design checklists alongside AI-generated outputs to identify representation gaps.
  •   Recognize that AI tools reflect the biases of their training data and require active human correction.
  • Intellectual Property and Licensing Concerns
  •   Review the terms of service of each AI tool to understand the ownership and licensing implications of generated outputs.
  •   Be cautious about incorporating AI-generated code into commercial or client work without appropriate legal review.
  •   Some AI tools provide provenance information indicating when suggestions are closely derived from specific training sources.
  •   Establish team or organizational policies for acceptable use of AI-generated code in different project types.
  • Maintaining Professional Competence
  •   Develop and maintain foundational HCI and coding skills independent of AI tool assistance.
  •   Use AI tools to extend and accelerate competence rather than to substitute for developing it.
  •   Regularly practice tasks without AI assistance to maintain the ability to evaluate AI outputs critically.
  •   Seek feedback from experienced practitioners on AI-assisted work to identify skill gaps that AI may be masking.
  • Responsible AI Use in User-Facing Products
  •   Apply the same ethical scrutiny to AI-generated interface copy and interaction patterns as to human-authored work.
  •   Avoid using AI to scale production of dark patterns, manipulative design, or deceptive interfaces.
  •   Ensure that AI-generated data collection interfaces accurately represent what data is collected and how it is used.
  •   Center user wellbeing and autonomy as the primary design criteria when evaluating AI-generated outputs.
  Notes: Examines the ethical dimensions of using AI coding assistants in HCI work, including attribution, bias, intellectual property, and the professional responsibilities of designers who use AI tools. Encourages critical and reflective practice.
