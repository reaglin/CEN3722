Introduction

Every screen you design in Figma lives inside a frame. A frame is a container with its own boundaries, background, and layout rules, and it is what Figma treats as a "screen" when you move into prototyping. Learning to create, size, and nest frames well is the first practical step toward a prototype that behaves predictably rather than one that falls apart the moment you resize something.

On top of frames sit two tools that give your layouts structure: layout grids (columns, rows, and uniform grids that keep spacing and alignment consistent across screens) and constraints (rules that tell child objects how to anchor and stretch when their parent frame changes size). Device presets and safe areas connect these ideas to real hardware, so your mobile screen is actually the size of a phone and your important content is not hidden behind a notch or a home indicator. Finally, arranging frames on the canvas in a readable order turns a pile of screens into a flow that a reviewer or teammate can follow.

As you watch, pay attention to three things: where the presenter clicks to create or select a frame versus a group, how constraints are set in the right-hand panel and what changes when the frame is resized, and how grids are used to justify spacing decisions instead of eyeballing them. Try pausing and reproducing each step in your own file — these skills are muscle memory, and they are much faster to learn by doing than by watching.
