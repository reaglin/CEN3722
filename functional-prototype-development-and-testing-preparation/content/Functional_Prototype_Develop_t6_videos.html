Introduction

Up to this point in the module you have been building a functional prototype and drafting a user testing plan as if they were two separate deliverables. In practice they are one system: every task you ask a participant to perform must be supported by something that actually works in the prototype, with realistic data already loaded and the interface in a known starting state. Integrating prototype and testing preparation means walking through your protocol task by task and confirming that the build can carry it, trimming scope where it cannot, and deciding in advance what the prototype should look like the moment a participant sits down.

This topic covers three practical concerns. Aligning prototype scope with test tasks is about mapping each task script step to a screen, control, or flow that exists; anything unsupported either gets built, faked deliberately, or cut from the protocol. Preparing test data and prototype state covers seeding accounts, sample content, and default settings so sessions are comparable, plus the reset procedure between participants. Coordinating team roles across development and testing means naming who finishes the build, who facilitates, who takes notes, and who owns the pilot run that catches problems before real participants arrive.

The three videos below approach preparation from complementary angles: a research-practice checklist of pre-session work, a designer's end-to-end walkthrough of one usability study, and a set of best practices for running sessions well. While watching, keep your own project in mind and note anything that implies a prototype requirement — a piece of test data, a reset step, a role to assign, or a task you may need to narrow.
