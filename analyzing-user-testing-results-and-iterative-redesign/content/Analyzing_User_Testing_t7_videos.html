Introduction

Usability work does not end when you finish testing or even when you ship a revised interface. The final professional step is documenting what you found, what you changed, and why — in a form that teammates, stakeholders, and future designers can actually use. That means usability testing reports that tie claims to evidence, before-and-after comparisons that make the change visible, and metrics tracked consistently across iterations so improvement (or regression) is measurable rather than anecdotal.

Communicating those outcomes is its own skill. The people who approve your redesign are often not interface specialists, so findings must be framed in terms they care about: task success, time on task, error rates, user frustration, and business consequences. At the same time, you have an ethical obligation not to overstate what a small study shows. Selective quoting, cherry-picked clips, and percentages drawn from five participants can mislead an audience that has no way to check your work.

The three short videos below come from Nielsen Norman Group, a long-standing usability research and consulting firm. As you watch, pay attention to three things: how they define usability in measurable components you can report on, how they recommend handling participant quotes so they illustrate rather than distort, and what responsible presentation of study data looks like when the sample is small. Try mapping each recommendation to a section you would write in your own redesign report.

About this video

This video focuses on using participant quotes when communicating research insights, offering three practical tips for selecting and preparing them. It suggests that raw quotes usually need editing for clarity and context, and that quotes work best as support for a finding rather than as the finding itself.

Why this video

Quotes are the most common qualitative evidence in a usability report, and this video gives concrete guidance on using them persuasively without distorting participant meaning — useful for both report writing and stakeholder presentations.

How These Videos Support the Learning Outcomes

Together these videos support the module outcomes by supplying both the vocabulary and the communication practices that turn analysis into documentation.
