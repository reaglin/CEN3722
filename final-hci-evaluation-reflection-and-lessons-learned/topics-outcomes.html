Module Topics

Connecting HCI Theory to Design Outcomes

Examines how foundational HCI concepts and frameworks directly shaped design decisions and final product quality throughout the course.
  • Role of HCI Theory in Design Decisions — HCI theory provides the conceptual foundation that guides designers in making informed, user-centered choices at every stage of the design process.
  • From Concepts to Real-World Application — Translating abstract HCI concepts into concrete design artifacts requires deliberate effort to bridge theory and practice.
  • Evaluating Theory Alignment in Final Products — Assessing the final product for alignment with HCI theory reveals how well the team integrated course concepts into their design.

Review of the Analysis Phase

Reflects on how user research, task analysis, and requirements gathering during the analysis phase shaped the direction and quality of the final design.
  • User Research Methods and Their Impact — The user research methods chosen at the start of a project establish the foundation of empathy and understanding that informs every subsequent design decision.
  • Task Analysis and User Needs Definition — Task analysis translates raw user research into structured representations of what users need to accomplish and how they currently do so.
  • Requirements Gathering and Scope Management — Well-defined functional and non-functional requirements provide a shared understanding between team members and serve as a benchmark for design success.

Review of the Prototyping Phase

Examines the role of low-fidelity and high-fidelity prototyping in exploring design alternatives, communicating ideas, and facilitating early user feedback.
  • Low-Fidelity Prototyping and Ideation — Low-fidelity prototypes such as sketches and paper mockups enable rapid exploration of design concepts without significant investment of time or resources.
  • High-Fidelity Prototyping and Design Refinement — High-fidelity prototypes closely simulate the look and behavior of the final product, enabling more realistic usability testing and stakeholder review.
  • Iteration Between Prototype Versions — Effective prototyping is not linear; it involves cycling between design, testing, and refinement based on user and stakeholder feedback.

Review of the Evaluation Phase

Reflects on the usability evaluation methods employed, the quality of the feedback gathered, and how findings were translated into design improvements.
  • Usability Testing Methods — Usability testing exposes how real users interact with a prototype or product, revealing problems that designers and team members cannot detect through self-review alone.
  • Heuristic and Expert Evaluation — Heuristic evaluation involves experts reviewing a design against established usability principles to identify violations without requiring user participants.
  • Translating Evaluation Findings into Design Changes — The value of any evaluation method is only realized when findings are systematically analyzed and translated into concrete design improvements.
  • Measuring Design Quality Against Initial Goals — Evaluating the final product against the user needs and requirements established during the analysis phase closes the loop on the design process.

Structured Feedback on Design Quality

Covers the process of receiving, interpreting, and applying structured feedback on the final product's interface design, interaction patterns, and adherence to HCI principles.
  • Dimensions of Design Quality in HCI — Design quality in HCI is multi-dimensional, encompassing usability, accessibility, aesthetic integrity, and consistency across the user experience.
  • Providing and Receiving Constructive Critique — Structured critique is a professional skill that enables teams to give and receive feedback that is specific, evidence-based, and actionable.
  • Using Feedback to Identify Design Patterns — Recurring themes across multiple feedback sources indicate systematic design patterns—either strengths to preserve or weaknesses to address.

Process Execution and Team Collaboration

Reflects on how the team managed the design process over time, including planning, role distribution, communication, and conflict resolution.
  • Design Process Planning and Execution — A structured design process plan helps teams allocate time and resources effectively across research, prototyping, and evaluation phases.
  • Role Distribution and Contribution — Clearly defined roles within a design team prevent duplication of effort, ensure all competencies are utilized, and promote individual accountability.
  • Communication and Conflict Resolution — Effective team communication and proactive conflict resolution are essential for maintaining momentum and psychological safety throughout a design project.
  • Collaboration Tools and Documentation Practices — Digital collaboration tools and consistent documentation practices enable distributed teams to work coherently and maintain a shared record of design decisions.

Reflection and Lessons Learned

Guides teams and individuals through a structured reflection on what worked, what did not, and what they would do differently, consolidating learning for future application.
  • Individual Reflective Practice — Individual reflection encourages each team member to assess their personal growth, skill development, and contribution to the project.
  • Team-Level Lessons Learned — A team lessons-learned session synthesizes individual reflections into shared insights about process, collaboration, and design outcomes.
  • Connecting Course Concepts to Project Experience — Synthesizing the connection between course concepts and lived project experience cements learning and prepares students to transfer knowledge to new contexts.
  • Actionable Improvements for Future Projects — Lessons learned only create lasting value when translated into specific, actionable commitments for how future projects will be approached differently.
