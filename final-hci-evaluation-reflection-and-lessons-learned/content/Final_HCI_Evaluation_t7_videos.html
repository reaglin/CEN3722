Introduction

Reflection is the step that turns a finished project into durable learning. Now that your interface has been built, evaluated, and presented, this topic asks you to look backward with discipline: what design decisions held up under user testing, which assumptions about your users were wrong, where did ergonomic, psychological, cultural, or accessibility considerations surprise you, and what would you do differently if you started the project again tomorrow? Reflection here is not a vague feelings exercise — it is a structured, evidence-based critique of your own process.

The topic has two levels. Individual reflective practice asks you to write honestly about your own contributions, skills, and blind spots, supported by concrete evidence from your design artifacts and evaluation data. Team-level lessons learned asks the group to meet, surface what worked and what did not without blaming individuals, and record outcomes that a future team could actually act on. Both levels should connect explicitly back to course concepts — usability heuristics, user-centered design, accessibility standards, cognitive load, and the evaluation methods you used.

As you watch, look for structure: how a reflective statement moves from description to analysis to action, how a lessons-learned meeting is facilitated so that criticism stays constructive, and how reflection is built into project-based HCI work rather than tacked on at the end. Your goal is to come away with a small number of specific, actionable improvements you could carry into your next design project.
