## Background

Before any HCI design work begins, a project team must define the boundaries of what they are — and are not — going to do. This is called **project scoping**. A well-scoped project has clear goals, a defined audience, a justified platform choice, and a realistic set of constraints that the team has acknowledged and planned around.

In this assignment, you will apply scoping concepts from the module to your own project idea. You will select one of four target platforms — **Android**, **iOS**, **Windows**, or **Web** — and document your reasoning for that choice. You will also identify the design and development tools your team will use.

## Task Description

Work as a group to complete all steps below. Every team member should contribute to the discussion and decisions. The final deliverable is a single **Formal Project Description Document** submitted on behalf of the whole group.

### Step 1: Agree on a Project Idea

As a team, decide on the application you want to design this semester. Your application should:

- Solve a real problem or fulfill a genuine user need
- Be realistic in scope for one semester of academic work
- Be suitable for one of the four platforms covered in this course: Android, iOS, Windows, or Web

Discuss several ideas before committing. Rule out ideas that are too large, too vague, or dependent on technology your team cannot realistically learn within the semester.

### Step 2: Identify Your Target User Audience

Decide who will use your application. Be specific. "Everyone" is not an acceptable answer.

Consider:
- Age range and general technical proficiency
- The context in which they will use the app (at work, at home, on the go, etc.)
- The devices they are most likely to own and use
- Any accessibility needs that should influence your design

Your user audience description should be specific enough that it directly informs your platform choice in Step 3.

### Step 3: Select Your Target Platform

Based on your project idea and target user audience, select **one** of the following platforms:

- **Android** (mobile)
- **iOS** (mobile)
- **Windows** (desktop)
- **Web** (browser-based)

You must justify your choice using evidence about your users and the capabilities your application requires. Personal preference is not sufficient justification. Address the following in your justification:

- Where does your target audience actually use technology? What devices do they have?
- Does your application require any hardware features (camera, GPS, etc.) that favor one platform?
- Are there any team skill or tool constraints that affect feasibility?

> **Note on mobile projects:** If you are considering a mobile application, you must choose either Android *or* iOS as your primary platform — not both. Document why you chose one over the other.

### Step 4: Define Your Project Goals

Write a clear list of project goals. Distinguish between:

- **User-facing goals** — what the user should be able to *do* with your application (e.g., "Users can log and track their daily water intake")
- **Project goals** — what your team aims to *deliver* (e.g., "We will produce a high-fidelity interactive prototype of the core user flows")

You should have a minimum of **three user-facing goals** and **two project goals**. Goals should be specific and, where possible, measurable.

### Step 5: Define Your Project Constraints

Identify the real limitations your team is working within. Be honest. Constraints are not failures — acknowledging them leads to better-scoped projects.

Consider constraints such as:

- Semester timeline and major deadlines
- Team size and distribution of skills
- Access to required hardware (e.g., do all team members have a Mac if targeting iOS?)
- Access to required software or licenses
- Technical limitations of the chosen platform
- Features or functionality you are explicitly *not* including and why

You should identify a minimum of **four constraints**.

### Step 6: Select Your Design and Development Tools

Identify the specific tools your team will use. You must address both categories:

- **Design and prototyping tools** — the tool(s) you will use to wireframe and prototype your application (e.g., Figma, Adobe XD)
- **Development tools** — the IDE, framework, or code editor your team will use if the project proceeds to implementation (e.g., Android Studio with Kotlin, Xcode with Swift, Visual Studio Code for web)

For each tool, briefly state why your team chose it and confirm that all team members have access to it.

### Step 7: Write Your Formal Project Description Document

Compile everything from Steps 1–6 into a single, organized document using the structure defined in the **Required Output** section below.

## Required Output

Your submission must be a single document containing all of the following sections. Each section is required. Missing sections will result in lost marks as described in the grading rubric.

---

### Required Section 1: Project Title and Team Information

> **Include:**
> - A working title for your application
> - Team name (if applicable)
> - Full name and student ID of every team member
> - Identification of the team lead for this assignment (rotating roles are encouraged across the project series)

---

### Required Section 2: Application Concept Summary

> **Include:**
> - A clear description of what your application does (2–4 sentences)
> - The problem it solves or the need it fulfills
> - A brief statement of what makes this application appropriate for HCI design study

---

### Required Section 3: Target User Audience

> **Include:**
> - A specific description of your primary user group
> - Relevant characteristics (age range, technical proficiency, use context, device habits)
> - A statement connecting your user audience to your platform choice

---

### Required Section 4: Platform Selection and Justification

> **Include:**
> - Your selected platform (Android, iOS, Windows, or Web)
> - A structured justification covering: (a) user audience alignment, (b) platform capability alignment with your required features, and (c) team skill and tool feasibility
> - An explicit statement of any platforms you considered but ruled out, and why

---

### Required Section 5: Project Goals

> **Include:**
> - A numbered list of at least **three user-facing goals**
> - A numbered list of at least **two project goals**
> - Each goal should be written as a complete, specific sentence

---

### Required Section 6: Project Constraints

> **Include:**
> - A numbered list of at least **four constraints**
> - For each constraint, write one to two sentences explaining what the constraint is and how your team plans to work within it

---

### Required Section 7: Project Scope Boundary Statement

> **Include:**
> - A clear list of features or functionality that **are** within scope
> - A clear list of features or functionality that **are explicitly out of scope**
> - This section defines the boundary of your project. Be specific. "We will not build a back-end server" is a valid out-of-scope statement.

---

### Required Section 8: Design and Development Toolchain

> **Include:**
> - Your chosen design and prototyping tool(s) with a brief rationale
> - Your chosen development tool(s) and technology stack with a brief rationale
> - Confirmation that all team members have access to each tool listed
> - Any cross-platform tools or frameworks you plan to use, if applicable

---

### Required Section 9: Team Agreement Statement

> **Include:**
> - A short paragraph (3–5 sentences) in which the team collectively affirms that all members have reviewed and agreed to the project scope as documented
> - The full name of each team member, confirming their agreement (typed names are acceptable)

## AI Use Policy

AI tools — including but not limited to ChatGPT, Microsoft Copilot, Google Gemini, and similar large language model tools — are **permitted** for this assignment under the following conditions:

**Permitted uses:**
- Brainstorming application ideas or user audience characteristics
- Drafting and refining the language of your project description
- Checking grammar, clarity, and readability of your written sections
- Asking AI to explain platform characteristics or design conventions from the module

**Not permitted uses:**
- Submitting AI-generated content as your team's own reasoning without review or modification
- Using AI to make decisions your team has not actually discussed and agreed upon
- Fabricating team agreement or attributing views to team members without their knowledge

**Disclosure requirement:**
If your team used AI tools during this assignment, include a brief note at the end of your document stating which tools were used and for what purpose. This disclosure will not affect your grade. Omitting disclosure when AI was used is an academic integrity violation.

The decisions documented in this assignment — your platform choice, your goals, your constraints — must reflect actual group discussion and agreement. AI can help you *express* those decisions clearly; it cannot make them for you.
