Introduction

When you submit a group development report, your instructor is reading two things at once: the quality of the interface you designed, and the quality of the process your team used to get there. This topic focuses on the second part — how to document who did what, how decisions were made collectively, and how the project was planned and tracked over the term. Good collaboration documentation is specific and verifiable: it names roles, points to artifacts (meeting notes, task boards, design critiques, version histories), and explains how individual contributions fit into the whole.

There are four things a strong report usually covers. First, team roles and responsibilities — who owned research, wireframing, prototyping, usability testing, and writing, and how overlaps were handled. Second, the collaborative design process — how the team moved from research to sketches to prototypes to evaluation, and where group critique or shared decision-making changed the design. Third, project management activities — scheduling, task assignment, milestones, risks, and how scope was adjusted. Fourth, reflection — an honest account of what worked in the group process and what you would do differently.

The videos below come at this from two angles. Two are project management tutorials that give you concrete, reusable structures for recording responsibility and project scope; the third walks through a UX design process end to end, which is the kind of narrative your report needs to tell. As you watch, pay less attention to the specific tools being shown and more to the structure: what categories of information are captured, what level of detail is useful, and how each artifact could become a labeled section or table in your own group report.

About this video

A short, practical explanation of the RACI matrix — a chart that labels each person on a task as Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, or Informed. The video defines the four roles and, based on its description, walks through worked examples of assigning task roles across a project team. It is aimed at project managers who need to remove ambiguity about ownership.

Why this video

RACI is one of the cleanest formats for the "Documenting Team Roles and Responsibilities" subtopic, since it forces you to distinguish doing the work from owning the outcome — exactly the distinction a group report needs to make. TeamGantt is an established project management software company, so the treatment is standard and industry-aligned rather than idiosyncratic.

About this video

This tutorial explains what a project brief is, how it is used in project management, and what sections it should contain, with a downloadable template and examples referenced in the description. Expect coverage of elements such as objectives, scope, deliverables, stakeholders, timeline, and success criteria. It is pitched at someone writing the document for the first time.

Why this video

It models the structured, section-based technical writing your development report requires, and the brief's components map directly onto the project management activities (scope, milestones, deliverables) you are asked to document. Having a concrete template makes it easier to see what level of detail counts as professional documentation.

A designer narrates the UX design process step by step using a real mobile app project as the running example, moving through the typical stages from problem framing and research to wireframes, prototypes, and refinement.
