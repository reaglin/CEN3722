TILT Framework

📌 Purpose — Why This Assignment Matters

Before a single line of code is written or a single frame is designed, professional product teams invest time in one critical activity: getting organized. Industry research consistently shows that the number one cause of failed software projects is not technical failure — it is miscommunication, unclear ownership, and misaligned expectations within teams.

This assignment mirrors the real-world project kickoff ritual used by cross-functional product teams at companies like Google, Spotify, and Microsoft. You are not just completing a course assignment; you are practicing the professional ritual of standing up a team and a project from zero.

You and your team are embarking on something ambitious: designing, building, and publishing a commercial-quality software product — a real application that will go through the full publication cycle into a public app store or web platform. That ambition starts here, with alignment.

This submission connects directly to the following module topics:

  • Introduction to HCI — shared vocabulary and mental models for the work ahead
  • Version Control with GitHub — establishing ownership of the team repository
  • Design Collaboration with Figma — establishing ownership of the team design workspace
  • Team Collaboration and Project Kickoff — all elements of this assignment draw directly from this topic
  • AI Tools in HCI and Development — you will document your team's agreed approach to AI use

📋 Task — What You Will Do

Follow the step-by-step instructions in the Detailed Task Description section below. Your team will produce a single, structured kickoff document that covers four areas: team composition, leadership structure, account ownership, and communication protocol.

📊 Criteria — How It Will Be Graded

Your submission will be evaluated using the rubric at the end of this document. Graders will look for completeness, clarity, and evidence that your team has had a real conversation — not that anyone has produced polished prose.

Learning Outcomes

By the end of this assignment, you will be able to:

1. Construct a team charter that documents roles, responsibilities, and working norms for a cross-functional software project team.

2. Assign clear ownership of shared project infrastructure (GitHub repository, Figma workspace) using role definitions grounded in industry practice.

3. Design a team communication and project management protocol that supports transparent, accountable collaboration throughout the course.

4. Select and formally commit to a course project from a defined list of product ideas, producing an initial problem statement aligned with HCI principles.

Detailed Task Description

Work through the following steps as a team, synchronously (in person, on a video call, or in a shared chat session). This is not a document one person fills out alone. Every team member should be present and contribute to every decision.

Step 1 — Meet as a Team and Introduce Yourselves (10 minutes)

Each team member should briefly share:

  • Their name and preferred name/pronouns (if they wish to share)
  • Their strongest technical or creative skill relevant to this project (e.g., "I've built React apps before," "I'm comfortable with Illustrator and visual design," "I've managed GitHub repos for class projects")
  • One skill or area they want to grow in during this course
  • Their general weekly availability (e.g., "I'm free most evenings after 6 PM")

> Define "skill": For this step, a skill is any prior experience, coursework, tool familiarity, or personal project that is relevant to design, development, or project coordination. There is no minimum or maximum — be honest about where you are starting.

Record the following in your kickoff document:

For each team member, include:

`

Name:

Strongest relevant skill(s):

Area they want to grow in:

Weekly availability:

`

Step 2 — Assign Team Roles (10 minutes)

Your team will operate with four defined roles. These roles mirror cross-functional product team structures used in industry. Each role must be assigned to exactly one person. On larger teams (5+ members), two roles may be shared by two people — document this clearly.

| Role | Responsibilities |

|---|---|

| Project Lead | Coordinates tasks and deadlines, runs team check-ins, ensures the team stays on schedule, communicates with the instructor on behalf of the team |

| Design Lead | Owns and maintains the team Figma workspace, leads UI/UX design decisions, manages design file organization |

| Dev Lead | Owns and maintains the team GitHub repository, establishes and enforces the branching strategy, reviews and merges pull requests |

| Documentation Lead | Maintains the project README, records meeting notes, ensures the team charter and project documents stay current |

> Note: Roles may rotate in later modules. This is your starting assignment, not a permanent title.

Record the following in your kickoff document:

`

Project Lead: [Name]

Design Lead: [Name]

Dev Lead: [Name]

Documentation Lead: [Name]

Role assignment rationale: [2–3 sentences explaining why the team assigned roles this way — reference the skills identified in Step 1]

`

Step 3 — Establish Account Ownership and Access (10 minutes)

Your team will share two primary platforms throughout this course: GitHub and Figma. You need to decide on one canonical team account or workspace for each and ensure every team member has appropriate access before this submission is complete.

#### GitHub

  • Decide which team member's GitHub account will host the team organization or repository. This person is your Dev Lead (assigned in Step 2).
  • All other team members must be added as collaborators with appropriate permissions before submission.
  • Create a team repository named using the format: hci-[your team name]-project (e.g., hci-team-nova-project).
  • Create at least three GitHub Issues before submitting — one for each of the remaining major kickoff tasks your team still needs to complete. This mirrors Agile/Scrum practice used in industry.

> What is a GitHub Issue? An Issue is a tracked task, question, or to-do item inside a GitHub repository. Issues can be assigned to team members, labeled, and closed when complete. They are a primary tool for transparent team accountability.

#### Figma

  • Decide which team member's Figma account will host the team education workspace. This is typically your Design Lead.
  • All other team members must be invited to the workspace with at least Editor access.
  • Create a project inside the workspace named: [Team Name] — HCI Course Project.

Record the following in your kickoff document:

`

GitHub Repository URL:

GitHub Repository Owner (Dev Lead):

Confirmed collaborators added (list names):

Figma Workspace Owner (Design Lead):

Figma Workspace/Project Name:

Confirmed members invited (list names):

Three GitHub Issues created (list issue titles and who they are assigned to):

1.

2.

3.

`

Step 4 — Define Your Communication and Project Management Protocol (10 minutes)

Decide as a team how you will communicate and coordinate work throughout the course. Document your decisions clearly enough that a new team member joining in week two could read this and know exactly how your team operates.

Answer each of the following questions in your kickoff document:

1. Primary communication channel: What tool will your team use for day-to-day messaging? (Examples: Slack, Discord, Microsoft Teams, GroupMe, iMessage.) Specify the tool and the name of the channel or group.

2. Meeting cadence: How often will your team meet, and for how long? When are your standing meeting times? (Example: "Every Tuesday at 7 PM on Zoom, 30 minutes.")

3. Decision-making process: How will your team make decisions when there is disagreement? (Example: "We will discuss in the group chat for 24 hours, then take a majority vote. The Project Lead breaks ties.")

4. Missed deadline protocol: What happens if a team member cannot complete their assigned task on time? What is the expected notice period, and how will the team respond?

5. AI tool usage agreement: Your team must agree on and document a shared policy for using AI tools (such as GitHub Copilot, ChatGPT, Figma AI features, etc.) consistent with the course guidelines described in the AI Use Policy section of this document. At minimum, address: Which AI tools are permitted? How will AI usage be documented (e.g., in commit messages, design notes)? What is every team member's individual responsibility when submitting AI-assisted work?

Step 5 — Select Your Course Project and Write an Initial Problem Statement (10 minutes)

As a team, select one product idea from the list at the bottom of this document. You will design, build, and publish this product as a commercial-quality application by the end of the course.

Once you have selected your product, write an initial problem statement using the following structure:

> Our users are [describe who will use this product — be specific].

> They face the problem of [describe the real-world problem or friction point the product addresses].

> A successful solution would [describe what the product does and what "done" looks like from a user's perspective].

> We will build this as a [Windows Application / Web Application / Android Application / iOS Application].

> What makes a good problem statement? A strong problem statement is specific enough to guide design decisions. "People need a better app" is too vague. "College students living off-campus struggle to coordinate grocery runs with roommates because there is no shared, real-time list tool that integrates with their existing messaging apps" is appropriately specific.

Record the following in your kickoff document:

`

Selected Product:

Platform (circle one): Windows App | Web App | Android App | iOS App

Initial Problem Statement: [Write your problem statement using the structure above]

`

AI Use Policy

AI tools are encouraged in this course as productivity aids — they are part of the real-world HCI and development toolkit you are learning to use. However, the following rules apply to this and all course submissions:

For this specific assignment:

AI may be used to help draft language, suggest team charter structures, or generate initial problem statement ideas. However, all decisions documented in your kickoff — roles, tools, communication norms, product selection — must reflect real conversations your team has had. You may not use AI to invent team members' skills, fabricate consensus, or skip the team discussion entirely.

For the course overall (document your team's policy in Step 4):

  • You are responsible for understanding, reviewing, and standing behind all AI-assisted work you submit.
  • You must be able to explain any design decision or line of code, even if AI helped generate it.
  • AI usage should be documented in commit messages (e.g., feat: generated button component with Copilot, reviewed and modified) and in Figma design notes where applicable.
  • AI is a collaborator, not a substitute for understanding.

Accessibility

  • All instructions in this assignment are written in plain language. If any term or instruction is unclear, contact the instructor or post in the course Q&A forum.
  • If your team's submitted document contains images, screenshots, or diagrams, include descriptive alt text or a written caption for each image.
  • If any team member requires accommodations that affect participation in synchronous team activities (e.g., real-time video calls), please contact the instructor so that asynchronous participation alternatives can be arranged.
  • Submitted PDFs should be exported from your word processor rather than photographed or scanned to ensure screen-reader compatibility.
