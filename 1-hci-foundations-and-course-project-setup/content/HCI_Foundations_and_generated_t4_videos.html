Introduction

Artificial intelligence has moved from a research curiosity into the everyday toolkit of designers and developers. Generative models now help teams brainstorm interface concepts, draft wireframes and copy, generate and refactor code, write tests, and summarize user research. In this topic we survey where these tools actually fit in the design and development workflow, what they do well, and where human judgment still has to carry the work.

This matters for CEN3722 because your course project will run through the same lifecycle these tools touch: scoping and planning, sketching and prototyping, building, and evaluating. Knowing which tasks an AI assistant can accelerate — and which ones it tends to make worse, such as understanding real users, justifying design decisions, or ensuring accessibility — will make you a faster and more credible team member. It also sets up the course policy on responsible use: AI assistance is a tool you disclose and verify, not a substitute for your own reasoning or a source you can cite blindly.

As you watch the videos below, pay attention to three things. First, which stage of the workflow is each tool being applied to (ideation, visual design, coding, documentation)? Second, what verification step does the presenter take — or fail to take — after the AI produces output? Third, notice how quickly specific tools and features change; the underlying workflow patterns will outlast any particular product shown on screen.

How These Videos Support the Learning Outcomes

These videos sit alongside the module's conceptual material rather than replacing it. By showing where AI fits into ideation, visual design, and implementation, they help you see the distinction between surface-level interface polish and the deeper usability, user experience, and accessibility concerns that AI output does not automatically satisfy (MO2) — a gap you should be able to name after watching. The Figma and workflow guides are most directly useful for MO3: they give you a realistic sense of what your team can produce and how fast, which informs the scope, platform choice, and role assignments in your initial project plan. The coding-assistant video similarly helps you estimate development effort when dividing responsibilities. Throughout, treat the tools shown as current examples of a longer HCI trajectory of making interface work easier and more accessible (MO1), and apply the course's disclosure-and-verification expectations to anything you generate.
