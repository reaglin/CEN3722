Introduction

Choosing iOS as a target platform means accepting a particular design philosophy along with a particular set of rules. Apple publishes the Human Interface Guidelines (HIG), maintains a consistent system of controls, typography, materials, and navigation patterns, and reviews every app before it reaches the App Store. For an HCI designer, that combination is both a gift and a constraint: much of the interaction vocabulary is already decided for you, users arrive with strong expectations about how things should behave, and deviating from convention tends to cost you more than it gains.

In this topic we look at three things: what the iOS platform actually is (devices, system design language, and the audience that uses it), the design conventions that make an app feel "native" on iOS, and the constraints that come with Apple's more controlled ecosystem — review policies, hardware and device-size assumptions, accessibility expectations, and limited freedom to invent your own UI patterns. These factors feed directly into whether iOS is the right platform for your team's project.

The videos below come from Apple Developer's WWDC sessions, which is as close to a primary source as you can get for iOS design. While watching, note the recurring vocabulary — hierarchy, consistency, materials, controls, navigation — and ask yourself two questions: what does this guidance require of a designer, and what would it prevent you from doing? Those answers become constraints you can cite in your scoping document.
