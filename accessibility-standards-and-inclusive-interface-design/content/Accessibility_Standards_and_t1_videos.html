Introduction

The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) are the internationally recognized standard for making digital content usable by people with disabilities. Published by the World Wide Web Consortium (W3C), WCAG is organized around four principles — Perceivable, Operable, Understandable, and Robust (the POUR principles) — which break down into guidelines, and then into testable success criteria. Those criteria are grouped into three conformance levels: A (minimum), AA (the level most commonly required by law and policy), and AAA (the most demanding).

In this course, WCAG gives you a shared, checkable vocabulary for talking about interface quality. Instead of saying a design "feels hard to use," you can point to a specific success criterion — contrast, keyboard access, focus visibility, target size — and explain exactly what must change. This matters practically as well: WCAG 2.0, 2.1, and 2.2 AA are referenced by accessibility laws and procurement requirements in many countries, so knowing the structure and versions is part of professional competence.

As you watch the videos below, focus on three things: how a success criterion is worded and why that wording makes it testable, how the version numbers (2.0, 2.1, 2.2) build on one another rather than replacing one another, and how individual criteria map back to the POUR principles. Keep a running list of criteria you could actually check on an interface you have built — you will need that skill for the module assignments.

How These Videos Support the Learning Outcomes

Together these videos build the standards literacy MO1 requires: the overview establishes WCAG's structure and conformance levels, the version comparison situates WCAG 2.1 among its neighbors, and the 2.2 summary demonstrates concrete, testable criteria. The specific criteria discussed — focus visibility, target size, and related requirements — give you a checklist mindset for detecting visual, motor, and cognitive barriers in an existing interface (MO2) and for revising prototypes to meet a defined conformance level (MO3). Because WCAG AA is the benchmark cited in accessibility law and policy, understanding it also grounds the ethical and legal reasoning called for in MO4.
